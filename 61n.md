# 61N Hackathon Challenge

## Challenge Statement
Enable security organizations to be aware and access civilian resources across the nation to support operations.

In Finland, the concept of "kokonaisturvallisuus" — comprehensive security — underpins national resilience by ensuring that the entire society contributes to preparedness and defense. In a heightened security environment, this requires the ability to quickly find, mobilize, and coordinate all available mission-critical resources, both military and civilian. Civilian networks hold vital assets such as equipment, infrastructure, and skills significantly benefiting local defense and crisis response. By developing a tool for mapping, combining, and managing civilian contributions, comprehensive security can be strengthened and security, as well as safety organizations, can take advantage of all available resources to increase their capabilities.

## The Challenge
Create a prototype system that allows real-time mapping of civilian-provided resources (e.g., vehicles, generators, construction equipment, specialized skills) to authorities upon heightened tension or active military conflict. The solution should allow authorities and communities to gather and visualize local resource availability, as well as plan and coordinate the allocation of these resources effectively in a rapidly evolving environment. Additionally, the solution should enable proactive civilian participation in resource provision. Participants are asked to design a lightweight, user-friendly solution supported by a backend (map library, database, input forms, automated data gathering, etc.) that prioritizes resilience, availability under crisis conditions, and adaptability to security-sensitive contexts.

## Initial Analysis
We would like to see solutions that allow civilian-owned resources and skills — such as vehicles, generators, medical skills, and shelters — to be confidentially and securely registered, verified, and visualized on a map in near real time during a military crisis. We are especially interested in lightweight, resilient prototypes that can function with limited connectivity, use simple input forms, and provide an intuitive overview for both local communities and authorities. The first solution rarely is the last one, so make sure your solution is modular and maintainable.

Example problems to consider:
- How to gather and visualize resources
- How collaboration between civilians and security organizations around these resources should be implemented
- What is the best way to enable resource requests from security organizations towards civilians
- How to optimize the allocation of known resources

## Other Important Details
- The challenge is designed so that no software, data, or assets are needed — purely greenfield.
- In a heightened security environment, society runs normally despite political and military tensions. The threat of escalation is well recognized, and policymakers are determined to act if needed. Military and security authorities are on elevated alert, but mobilization has not started. Surprising or unconventional events are possible, and news about foreign provocations such as cyber-attacks are part of everyday life.
